The Link Between Diverse Words and Original Ideas Is Weakening in the AI-Era College Admissions

Across 372,793 college-admissions essays and three controlled experiments, AI use increased surface-level word diversity while reducing conceptual diversity—a pattern we call Semantic Disjunction.

Kibum Moon, Kostadin Kushlev, Andrew Bank, Benjamin Lira Luttges, Indre Viskontas, James C. Kaufman, Dan Richard Johnson, Angela Lee Duckworth, and Adam E. Green

Key findings

More varied words did not mean more original ideas.

Surface diversity increased

Personal statements written after ChatGPT’s 2022 release used increasingly diverse words. Controlled experiments provided causal evidence that AI use can produce the same shift.

Conceptual diversity decreased

At the same time, ideas became more homogeneous both within essays, across sentences, and between whole documents. The usual positive association between diverse words and diverse ideas weakened or reversed in LLM writing.

Creativity judgments followed the surface

Human and AI raters perceived post-ChatGPT essays as more creative. Increased word-level diversity helped explain these judgments even when underlying concepts had become less diverse.

The pattern was not evenly distributed

Disjunctive trends were widespread, but the strongest changes appeared among minoritized applicants, raising questions about equity in high-stakes evaluation.

The core idea

What is Semantic Disjunction?

Semantic Disjunction is the separation of surface-level linguistic variety from the diversity of the ideas that language expresses. In ordinary human writing, richer vocabulary tends to accompany more varied thinking. LLM assistance can optimize the surface independently, producing polished and lexically diverse language without a corresponding increase in conceptual originality.

In short: AI can make writing sound more diverse while making the underlying ideas more similar.

Abstract

Study summary

Concerns that AI homogenizes human thinking appear at odds with findings that LLM writing is perceived as more creative than human writing. We propose that LLMs enhance surface-level diversity while simultaneously homogenizing underlying ideas. Four large-scale natural experiments tested this preregistered “semantic disjunction” hypothesis in the high-stakes context of college admissions. Comparing personal statements written by 372,793 applicants after-versus-before ChatGPT’s 2022 release, multi-level semantic analyses revealed increasing surface diversity of words simultaneous with decreasing conceptual diversity of sentences (within essays) and whole documents (between essays). Three controlled experiments provided causal evidence that AI use produced semantic disjunction, including in applicants from the natural experiment, and indicated an apparent root mechanism: a positive association between surface and conceptual diversity in humans was weakened or reversed in LLM writing. Despite conceptual homogenization, raters perceived post-ChatGPT essays as more creative. Semantic disjunction explained this apparent paradox: increasing word-level diversity explained increasing creativity judgments, overriding effects of decreased conceptual diversity, even when raters (human and AI) considered many essays together. Avoiding over-reliance on word-level surface diversity by considering multiple semantic levels is likely to support more complete understanding of AI’s effects on human thinking. Disjunctive trends were widespread, but were strongest in minoritized applicants.

Frequently asked questions

AI, creativity, and college essays

Does ChatGPT make college-admissions essays more similar?

The study found that essays written after ChatGPT’s release became more conceptually similar within and across documents, even while their word choices became more varied. Controlled experiments supported a causal role for AI use.

Why can AI writing appear more creative?

Readers use surface cues such as varied vocabulary when judging creativity. LLMs can strengthen those cues independently of the originality of the underlying ideas, making a text appear creative even as its concepts converge with other texts.

Does the research show that all AI-assisted writing is homogeneous?

No. The evidence concerns average shifts across large samples and controlled conditions. It does not imply that every AI-assisted essay is unoriginal or that every form of human–AI collaboration has the same effect.

How was semantic diversity measured?

The analyses examined multiple semantic levels: diversity among words, diversity among sentences within an essay, and diversity among whole documents. This multi-level approach makes it possible to detect the separation between surface variety and conceptual variety.

Why does Semantic Disjunction matter for admissions?

Admissions readers may over-rely on polished and varied language as a signal of originality. If that signal becomes less connected to idea diversity, evaluation practices may need to attend more directly to conceptual content and to unequal effects across applicant groups.
